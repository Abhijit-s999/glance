Open the reader

Read faster without moving your eyes.

Glance shows a book one word at a time, each word lined up on a red focus letter. Your eyes stay in one place while the words come to you, at whatever speed you set.

Open the reader Take the pace test

On a printed page, your eyes are always jumping.

Your eyes don't glide along a line of text. They stop on a word for about a quarter of a second, jump to the next stop, and every so often jump back to check something they missed.1 While they're moving, you aren't taking anything in.

Glance moves the words to you instead.

Each word is placed so that one letter, a little left of its middle, lands in the same spot every time. That's close to where studies find a word is recognised fastest.2 Your eyes rest there while the book goes by.

Spin the dial, or let a quick test choose.

Pick a speed with the dial and start reading. If you'd rather not guess, the pace test plays short passages and asks one question after each. It nudges you faster when you're keeping up and slower when you miss things, with smaller steps each time, until you settle on a speed you can follow.

You can change speed at any point while you read, and pause to see the whole paragraph with your place underlined.

What the research says, including the catch.

Showing words one at a time is a well-studied idea, usually called rapid serial visual presentation. It has real limits, and Glance is built around them.

Looking back helps you understand.

Readers who can't glance back at earlier words understand less of what they read.4

In Glance, pause at any moment and the paragraph you're in appears, with your place underlined. Click any word to carry on from there.

You blink less.

A study of the Spritz app found people blinked far less and reported more tired eyes.5

In Glance, the words linger a little at commas, full stops and new paragraphs, which leaves natural moments to blink.

Faster isn't always better.

In follow-up work, understanding started to slip above about 250 words a minute.6 Some people can go well past that, and some can't.

In Glance, the pace test asks a question after every passage, so the speed you keep is one you can actually follow. For dense or difficult books, turn the dial down.

Ten free books to start with.

Classics that are out of copyright, ready to read. Or bring your own: Glance opens text files, EPUBs and PDFs right in your browser. Spines are as wide as the book is long, and times are at the speed on your dial.

Your books stay with you.

Files are opened in your browser and kept there. There's no account, no upload and no tracking. Even the fonts are served from this site, so nothing about what you read goes anywhere else.

It works offline.

After your first visit the reader keeps working without a connection. Add it to your home screen and it opens like an app, ready on a train or a plane.
